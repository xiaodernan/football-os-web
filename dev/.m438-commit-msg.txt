M438: the rule census, extended to the bodies it could not see - the local helpers

M417's rule census reads EXPORTED bodies - a census of "no two exported bodies are the same rule
wearing two names" - and M422 found a family it structurally could not see: "one result credits
one row" was spelled in three LOCAL helpers, and a local copy of a door is exactly what that
census was blind to. The app has 118 local bodies against 52 exported ones: more than twice as
many rules live where the gate never looked.

The fix. The same strict-alignment measure now runs over EVERY named body - 170 of them, exported
or not - and the local families are closed on the same terms as the exported ones.

The seam, charged both ways. The new clause reads the local population and requires its families
to be closed: 118 local bodies, 1 same-rule pair, 0 undeclared, 0 stale. That one pair is a
DECLARED SPLIT, and it is worth naming: the Film Room removes a clip by its clipId and the
Learning Hub removes a record by its id - the same shape, two different contracts, and the measure
cannot tell them apart because it renames identifiers (a clip and a record are not the same row).
The reader is armed by a planted pair of local bodies, which reads 1, so the zero above is a
measurement and not a blind spot. The file passes 6/6.

What the extension found. Nothing new - and that is the finding: after M422's fold, the app's
local bodies are already converged under this measure. The value of the extension is REACH, not
a family: a local copy of a rule written tomorrow is caught tomorrow, in a place the census did
not look before, and the one pair the measure does pair is named rather than quietly excused.

Still outstanding. The measure pairs bodies that are the same rule TOKEN FOR TOKEN, so the
family M422 found is still invisible to it: four copies of the same rule, each shaped
differently, are four different token sequences. That is M420's lesson again - a census charges
the shapes its author could think of - and the honest measure there is not the text but the
DECISION: a comparison that answers the same question twice, whatever it is compared against. No
gate in the app measures that yet, and the two-arm and three-arm sign censuses are the closest
thing to it.
