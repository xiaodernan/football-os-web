M439: the sign of a difference, everywhere it is asked - the copies the door did not reach

M426 closed the door that says WHICH WAY a difference points, and left the copies behind. Four
places still asked the question by themselves:

- the CAREER SUMMARY - a best win (best.diff > 0) and a worst loss (worst.diff < 0), each spelled
  TWICE in the same function: once in the evidence lines and once in the record;
- the WEEKLY REVIEW - a scan trend's kind (f.scanTrend >= 0 ? "positive" : "watch");
- the FILM ROOM - a scan trend's colour (insights.scanTrend >= 0 ? "text-success" :
  "text-destructive");
- the COMPARISON PAGE - a verdict's side (adv > 0 ? "a" : "b").

So the review's scan trend and the Film Room's scan trend were THE SAME QUESTION - which way does
this trend point - asked in a domain module and in a screen, each with its own answer, and the
career summary asked it twice in one place.

The fold. All four ask deltaSign - the door - and the Film Room's colour rides the app's shared
difference table (DELTA_CLASS), so the three classes a difference wears are written once. The
review's kind keeps its own WORDS ("positive" and "watch" are the review's vocabulary, not the
app's) but asks the door for the DIRECTION, which preserves its rule exactly: a trend of zero is
not a fall, and "not down" is what the old >= 0 said.

The seam, charged both ways. proof-deltasign gained the TWO-ARMED shape - a comparison against
zero that comes FIRST - and one subtlety: a chain whose zero test is its LAST arm is a BAND, not
a sign (the seed's fixture status, "-2 days then 0 days then live"), and the reader refuses it. One
SPLIT is declared: the manager's MORALE, where a morale of exactly zero reads "good" while the
door's "flat" is neither up nor down - two different questions about two different quantities. The
file passes 5/5.

Still outstanding. The census can only see a sign WRITTEN AS A COMPARISON: a sign asked through a
call (fatigueIsHigh(x) ? ... : ...) is invisible to it, and so is a sign asked against a VARIABLE
(x > limit). The rule "the door owns the question" is therefore enforced on the shapes the reader
knows, and the shapes it does not know are exactly the ones a new author would reach for first.
