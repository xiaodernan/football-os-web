M448: the panel is written once too - the quiet box and the depth it wears

M447 closed the label family and left a boundary of its own: a wash of a SURFACE
colour is a PANEL, not a label - the app's quiet box, a border and a wash with no
word of its own. The census could name that boundary but could not charge it, and the
app had been drawing its boxes by hand the whole time. This folds the panel family
into a palette of its own.

The defect, measured before it was fixed. The app drew its quiet boxes in FIVE depths
of the same card surface - /30, /40, /50, /60 and the full bg-card - and FIFTY-NINE
sites across twenty-one screens spelled the wash out by hand. The smallest box, the
one a fact sits in ("rounded-md border bg-card/30 px-2 py-1.5"), was written THIRTEEN
times: four in the film room, four in the club detail, four in the simulation lab, one
in the manager OS. The softer panel ("rounded-lg border bg-card/40 p-3") was written
four more, and the app's own Panel, Mini, StatTile and EmptyState primitives each
spelled a depth of their own. So the shade a box wore was decided by whichever screen
wrote it first, and there was no single place in the app where a reader could see how
many depths the app had.

The fold. Eight entries joined a new PANEL_CLASS beside the chip palette, and the
fifty-nine sites now name it. The class SET is unchanged: an entry carries exactly the
colour classes the site wore - its wash and its border - and every other class the site
wore (its radius, its padding, its size) stays at the site, so a box is drawn exactly
as it was. A bare `border` IS the border colour in Tailwind (the width class with the
theme's border colour), so the reader sees it as a border and the panel's border rides
with its wash. The fold compared the class set before and after for every site and
refused to write a file whose set had changed; a separate reader re-derived all
fifty-nine sets from the committed files and found no change in any of them.

The seam, charged both ways. The census now reads a SEVENTH shape, the panel, and the
boundary around it is measured rather than assumed. A panel is a border and a wash of
one of the app's QUIET SURFACES - the card a page is made of, and the secondary a quiet
row sits on - with no word of its own, not even a word on hover. Four things are NOT
panels and the reader says so: a box with a WORD in it says something and is not a
panel; a wash of the SHELL's own surface (the modal's background, the sidebar) is the
shell's, not a panel the app composes; a SOFTENED border with a semantic wash is a
tinted container; and a solid button is not a label, because its word is the wash's own
foreground companion. The reader is planted with all four, so the zeros below are a
measurement and not a blind spot, and the panel clause is planted with a screen that
spells a panel out, so it is seen REFUSING rather than only reported clean. The panel
palette holds eight entries and the reader checks every panel the app wears against
them, so a panel written in the palette's own file but missing from the palette is
caught too.

The measurement, stated plainly. The five depths were NOT merged, and this unit says
why rather than assuming: each depth is used consistently for its own kind of box -
/30 for the small fact box, /40 for the soft panel, /50 for the app's own stat tiles,
/60 for the app's Panel, and the full card for a field row. There is no depth fighting
another; there are five kinds of box, and this unit gave each of them a name. Merging
them would flatten a hierarchy the app is drawing on purpose, and a milestone that
cannot say which of the two is right should not pick one.

Still outstanding. The census reads a class string, and it reads three colour roles -
border-, bg-, text- - plus the bare `border`. A RING ("ring-1 ring-primary/40", the
onboarding screen's selected mode) is a fourth role the reader does not see, and the
app's PROGRESS BAR is a fifth: a groove (bg-muted in eleven places, bg-secondary in
five, bg-sidebar in one) with a fill that wears bg-primary, bg-primary/50, bg-primary/60,
bg-success, bg-info, bg-warning or bg-destructive depending on what the bar measures. The
bar's own two halves are drawn apart, and the groove is the same groove in three
washes. That is the next family.
