M449: the solid is written once too - the wash and the word that reads on it

M448 closed the panel family and left the census reading three colour roles. There is
one shape left that the census can see and the app had not named: the SOLID - a wash
and the word that reads ON it, the wash's own "-foreground" companion, with no border
of its own. This folds it, and it corrects a misclassification the last three units
left behind.

The defect, measured before it was fixed. The app wore the primary solid
("bg-primary text-primary-foreground") on the settings page's chosen tab, the secondary
solid on the position tag, the shell's own two solids on its emblem and its active
mode, and the muted solid on the two result squares that carry a draw. Ten sites in
the app's own code spelled a wash and its companion by hand, and the DESTRUCTIVE solid
- the one the destructive button and the destructive badge wear - is written
"bg-destructive text-white": `text-white` is a colour THE THEME DOES NOT NAME, so the
census cannot see that solid at all, and neither can the palette. So the app's most
alarming colour was the one colour the app had never given a name.

The fold. Six entries joined the palette (solidPrimary, solidPrimarySoft,
solidSecondary, solidMuted, solidSidebar, solidSidebarPrimary) and the ten sites now
name it. The class SET is unchanged, and the fold compared the class set before and
after for every site and refused to write a file whose set had changed; a separate
reader re-derived all ten sets from the committed files and found no change in any of
them. One entry moved rather than appeared: the muted one had been filed as a LABEL
since M447, and this unit says why it was not one.

The seam, charged both ways. A LABEL is a wash and a word of its OWN colour - a tint
with its own colour on it. A SOLID is a wash and the COMPANION that stays legible on
it. The two are mirrors, and the companion is what tells them apart:
"bg-primary/15 text-primary" is a label, "bg-primary text-primary-foreground" is a
solid, and "bg-muted text-muted-foreground" is the second, because the muted colour's
companion IS the muted word - so M447's label entry was a solid wearing a label's
name, and this unit moved it. With that correction the two rules no longer overlap, and
the census reads EIGHT shapes with each one named. The reader is planted with the
solid alongside the label, the mark and the panel, and the solid's own clause is
planted with a screen that spells a solid out, so it is seen REFUSING rather than only
reported clean.

The scope, stated plainly. The starter's own components (components/ui) wear their own
solids - the button's four variants and the badge's four - and this unit LEFT THEM ALONE,
deliberately. The app's palette lives in ui/primitives.tsx, and that file already
imports the badge and the button; a palette the badge imported would close a loop from
ui/primitives back to ui/primitives, and the class the badge read would not be
initialised when the badge's own module ran. The app's palette must not depend on the
components that wear it. The clause says so and prints the count it left to the starter,
so the gap is measured rather than hidden.

The correction, measured. The census's shared-chip rule now reads the CHIP family - the
tone chip, the muted chip and the option at rest - and the families that came after it
each carry their own stricter rule. Before this unit the shared rule also read the
solids, and it fired on the starter's own button and badge; a rule that cannot tell the
app's copy from the template's is not a rule about the app.

Still outstanding. The destructive solid still wears a word the theme does not name, so
the theme has no --color-destructive-foreground to give it - the palette cannot hold a
colour the reader cannot see. And the app's PROGRESS BAR is still two halves drawn
apart: a groove that wears bg-muted in thirteen places, bg-secondary in five and
bg-sidebar in one, and a fill whose colour means something (bg-primary for progress,
bg-success for mastered, bg-info for confidence, bg-warning and bg-destructive for a low
bar). The bar's own Progress component wears a fourth track, bg-primary/20. That is the
next family, and it is the first one where the MEANING of a colour, not just its shade,
is the thing to name.
