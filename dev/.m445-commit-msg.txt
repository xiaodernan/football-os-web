M446: the badge is written once too - the border and the word that share a colour

M441 folded the chip - a border, a wash and a word, all of one colour - into one
palette, and named the family it did not fold: the BADGE, a border and a word of
one colour with no wash of its own. This closes that family.

The defect, measured before it was fixed. The amber badge
("border-warning/40 text-warning") was spelled out in seven places, the green
one ("border-success/40 text-success") in five, the strong amber
("border-warning/50 text-warning") in two, and the strong green, the strong blue
and the plain blue once each: seventeen spellings across ten screens, every one
of them a copy of the rest. A screen could wear a caution in one shade on one
page and another shade on the next, and the shade a badge wore was decided by
whichever screen happened to write it first.

The fold. Six entries joined the palette beside the chips (badgeSuccess,
badgeWarning, badgePrimary and the three strong forms), and the seventeen sites
now name the palette instead of spelling the classes. The class SET is
unchanged: the badge's own classes move to the end of the list and CSS does not
read the order of a class attribute, so nothing is painted differently. A site
that wore its badge with a size ("border-warning/40 text-[10px] text-warning")
keeps the size and wears the palette's colour; a site that wore the badge alone
wears the palette's entry and nothing else. The fold checked its own work: for
every site it compared the class set before and after and refused to write a
file whose set had changed.

The seam, charged both ways. The chip census now reads FOUR shapes, and the
fourth is the badge. A wash of the same colour makes it a chip again, and the
reader is planted with both so the boundary is measured rather than assumed. The
family has a clause of its own - every badge the app wears is written in the
palette alone - which is stricter than the chips' rule, because a badge was
never allowed to be a single screen's own. The palette now holds seventeen
entries (eleven chips and six badges), the reader checks all seventeen against
the text the app wore before the fold, and twenty-two screens are checked for
reading it. proof-chips 9/9; proof-record 5/5 (237 headings); proof-ledger-run
5/5; tsc --noEmit clean.

Still outstanding. The census charges a label that carries a border and a word.
It does not yet charge one that carries a wash and a word with no border - the
community hub's "yours" label ("bg-primary/15 text-primary") is that shape, and
the learning hub's right answer ("border-success bg-success/10", a border and a
wash with no word) is its mirror. Neither is visible to the reader today.
